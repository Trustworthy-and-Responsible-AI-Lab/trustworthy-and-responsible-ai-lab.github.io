Written Paper Critiques (20%)

  • Write a critique for one of the two papers assigned for each class. Do not choose the [Optional] papers.
  • Each critique must have: summary, contributions, strengths and weaknesses, and your opinions.
  • No late submissions are accepted. No critique is due for the first class (09/23)—so the first one is due 09/28.
  • Scored 0 / 1 / 2 points each; your total is scaled to the 20% above.

 

In-Class Paper Presentation (15%)

  • You present one of the two papers assigned for the day you signed up for. Up to 2 students may present the same paper together.
  • Sign up by 09/30 on the Google Sheet.
    • Sign-up is first come, first served: if no slot is left, you receive 0% for this component.
    • The same rule applies if you later need to move your date—you can only move into a slot that is still open.
  • You MUST meet Sanghyun once, about half a week before your presentation, to revise it.
    • Ask for the meeting in class or by email. Missing it costs 5%.
  • 25 min presentation + 10-25 min discussion that you lead.

Term Project (50%)

  • Teams of up to 3 students; no solo projects. Sign up on the Google Sheet by 09/30, or lose 2% of the final score.
  • Track 1 (Research): pursue an open research problem of your own choice.
  • Track 2 (Reproducing prior work): implement state-of-the-art techniques for preventing and tracing the distillation of large language models, and contribute them to a shared evaluation platform. The methods, models, and datasets are assigned by the instructor; the repository and assignments are shared after sign-up closes.
  • Three presentations:
    • Checkpoint 1 on 10/12 (15%)
    • Checkpoint 2 on 11/09 (15%)
    • Final showcase on 12/02, with the report (20% together)
  • Report due 12/09 on Canvas.

Final Exam (15%)

Course Policy

The University's Code of Academic Integrity applies, modified as follows.

[Don'ts]

  • Do NOT share your code or write-up with others.
  • Do NOT copy and paste someone else's code or write-up into yours.
  • Do NOT ask for the solutions on the Internet.

[Do's]

  • Brainstorm your ideas with other students.
  • Discuss and explain your solutions with other students.
  • Discuss the problems and concepts for the team project.
  • Collaborate with your team members to run experiments for the team project.

Must: Please write down the students' names if you received any help from them. It won't affect your scores, but you will learn how to credit others for their services.

AI Policy

The default in this course is that you do not use generative AI, and you must be able to explain and defend anything you submit.

  • Critiques: do NOT use AI to read, summarize, or write them. Polishing grammar in writing that is already yours is fine. Idea-level use — the summary, contributions, strengths and weaknesses, or opinions — earns 0 points and is reported to the School of EECS.
  • Final exam and term project: do NOT use AI. You are responsible for what you submit — "the AI produced it" is no defense if the text, code, or results are wrong or fabricated.
  • Disclosure: If you used AI at all, you MUST say so in your submission: the tool, the type of interaction, and what portion of the work it contributed to. Undisclosed use earns 0% for that component and is reported.

Unauthorized AI use may constitute plagiarism, cheating, and/or falsification under the University's Code of Student Conduct. If you are unsure whether a use is allowed, ask before you use it.
